Fishing Knot Library

Eight knots cover almost every situation. Tap each step to follow along, and use the chooser to pick the right knot for the job.

Palomar knot

Strength: Very highDifficulty: EasyLines: Braid, mono, fluoro

Simple, fast and very strong. Best with braid and smaller lures. It's awkward with big crankbaits because the loop has to pass over the whole lure.

  1. Double about 15 cm (6 in) of line and push the loop through the hook eye.
  2. Tie a loose overhand knot with the doubled line, letting the hook hang in the middle.
  3. Pass the loop completely over the hook or lure.
  4. Moisten, pull the tag end and main line together to tighten, then trim the tag.

Improved clinch knot

Strength: HighDifficulty: EasyLines: Mono, fluoro

The classic all-round knot for hooks, lures and swivels on monofilament. Not recommended for braid, which can slip.

  1. Thread the line through the eye and pull about 15 cm (6 in) through.
  2. Wrap the tag end around the standing line 5–7 times.
  3. Pass the tag end through the small loop just above the eye.
  4. Then pass it back through the big loop you just created.
  5. Moisten, pull the standing line to seat the coils, and trim.

Tip: use fewer wraps (5) on heavy line over 20 lb and more (7) on light line. Always wet the knot before cinching to avoid heat damage.
Terminal knot

Uni knot

Strength: HighDifficulty: EasyLines: All

The most versatile knot in fishing. One method gives you a terminal knot, a line joiner (double uni) and an arbor knot for spooling.

  1. Thread the line through the eye and double it back alongside the standing line.
  2. Make a loop with the tag end lying over the doubled lines.
  3. Wrap the tag through the loop and around both lines 5–7 times.
  4. Moisten and pull the tag to tighten the coils.
  5. Slide the knot down to the eye by pulling the standing line, then trim.

Loop option: stop before sliding the knot to the eye to leave an open loop, which gives lures like jerkbaits more action.
Line-to-line

Double uni knot

Strength: Good–highDifficulty: EasyLines: All, incl. braid to leader

A dependable, easy way to join two lines, including braid to fluorocarbon when you don't have time for an FG.

  1. Overlap the two line ends by about 20 cm (8 in).
  2. With one tag end, tie a uni knot around the other line: 5–6 turns for mono, 8–10 for braid.
  3. Repeat with the other tag end around the first line.
  4. Moisten, snug each knot, then pull the standing lines so the knots slide together. Trim both tags.

Braid tip: double the braid before tying its side of the knot for extra grip on slick fluorocarbon.
Line-to-line

FG knot

Strength: Very highDifficulty: AdvancedLines: Braid to leader

The slimmest, strongest braid-to-leader connection. It slides through rod guides cleanly, so it's worth the practice.

  1. Keep the braid under firm tension (for example around the reel and held in your teeth or fixed to the rod).
  2. Lay the leader across the braid and weave the braid around it, alternating over and under, 15–20 times.
  3. Pinch the wraps and secure them with 2–3 half hitches of braid around both lines.
  4. Pull everything very tight, trim the leader tag close, and finish with several half hitches around the braid only.

Test it: after tying, pull hard on the leader and braid with gloves on. A properly seated FG won't slip. If it does, add more wraps.
Terminal knot

Snell knot

Strength: HighDifficulty: MediumLines: Mono, fluoro

It binds the line to the hook shank so the hook rotates into the fish's jaw on the pull. Ideal for bait rigs and circle hooks.

  1. Pass the line through the hook eye toward the point, leaving a long tag along the shank.
  2. Hold the line along the shank so it forms a loop hanging below the hook.
  3. Wrap the loop tightly around the shank and the line 7–10 times, working toward the eye.
  4. Pull the standing line so the loop draws down under the wraps while you hold them in place.
  5. Tighten firmly against the hook and trim the tag.

Best for: live bait and cut bait with circle or octopus hooks, where a straight pull from the shank improves hook-ups.
Line-to-line

Surgeon's knot

Strength: GoodDifficulty: EasyLines: Mono, fluoro, tippet

The quickest way to join leader and tippet on the water, including lines of different diameters.

  1. Overlap the leader and tippet by about 15 cm (6 in).
  2. Form a loop with both lines held together.
  3. Pass the leader's tag end and the whole tippet through the loop twice (three times for a triple surgeon's).
  4. Moisten, pull all four ends evenly to tighten, and trim.

Loop knot

Perfection loop

Strength: GoodDifficulty: MediumLines: Mono, fluoro

A small, straight fixed loop for loop-to-loop connections on leaders and rigs.

  1. Make a loop behind the standing line, with the tag end passing behind.
  2. Make a second, smaller loop in front of the first by wrapping the tag around.
  3. Lay the tag end between the two loops.
  4. Pull the second loop through the first.
  5. Tighten by pulling the loop and the standing line, then trim the tag.

Pair it with: a fly line's welded loop or a pre-tied rig for fast, tool-free changes.
