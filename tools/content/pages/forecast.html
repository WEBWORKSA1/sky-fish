    How to read your fishing forecast

    The bite score (0–100) estimates how active fish are likely to be in each hour. Excellent (75+) hours usually line up with a solunar major period near dawn or dusk under favourable weather. Slow hours (under 45) are worth fishing, but slow down, scale down and fish tight to cover.

    Major and minor periods

    Major periods last about two hours and are centred on the moon passing directly overhead or underfoot. Minor periods last about an hour and are centred on moonrise and moonset. When a period overlaps sunrise or sunset, expect the strongest window of the day. Read the full explanation in Moon Phases & Solunar Theory.

    Pressure, wind and clouds

    We compare air pressure with three hours earlier. A slow fall (1–4 hPa) ahead of a front earns points, a steady barometer is neutral to good, and a sharp rise after a front costs points. Wind of 5–20 km/h breaks up the surface and helps. Gales hurt the score and your safety. See our pressure guide and reading the sky.

    Safety first: the forecast is for planning. Always check official marine forecasts, small-craft advisories, river levels and ice reports before you go.
