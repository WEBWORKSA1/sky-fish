Fish Species Library

Where they live, when they feed and what they eat, for the species anglers target most. Pair it with the forecast to plan the perfect window.

Freshwater

Largemouth Bass

Habitat
Weedy lakes, ponds, slow rivers; docks, laydowns, lily pads
Best season
Spring pre-spawn and fall feeding binge
Best time
Dawn, dusk and overcast days; nights in summer
Top baits
Soft plastic worms, jigs, spinnerbaits, topwater frogs
Pro tip

Largemouth relate to cover more than depth. In summer, find shade (mats, docks, deep weed edges) and slow down at midday. A falling barometer before a front is often the best bite of the week.

Freshwater

Smallmouth Bass

Habitat
Clear, rocky lakes and current-swept rivers
Best season
Late spring and early fall
Best time
Low light and windy days on main-lake points
Top baits
Tube baits, drop-shot, jerkbaits, crayfish
Pro tip

Smallmouth love rock and current. On windy days fish wind-blown points and shoals, where the chop disorients baitfish. In rivers, look for current seams behind boulders.

Freshwater

Rainbow Trout

Habitat
Cold streams, tailwaters and stocked lakes
Best season
Spring and fall; winter in tailwaters
Best time
Early morning, evening hatches, overcast days
Top baits
Nymphs, dry flies, small spinners, PowerBait (stocked)
Pro tip

Trout face upstream and hold where current delivers food without making them work: seams, riffle tail-outs and pocket water. Approach from downstream and keep your shadow off the water.

Freshwater

Brown Trout

Habitat
Rivers with undercut banks, deep pools; large cold lakes
Best season
Fall (pre-spawn aggression), spring
Best time
Dusk, night and dark, rainy days
Top baits
Streamers, minnow plugs, mouse patterns at night
Pro tip

Big browns are nocturnal predators. Stained water after rain and the last hour of light are your best chance at a trophy. Swing or strip streamers past undercut banks.

Freshwater

Walleye

Habitat
Large lakes and rivers; rock reefs, weed edges, current breaks
Best season
Spring after ice-out and fall
Best time
Low light: dawn, dusk and night (sensitive eyes)
Top baits
Jig and minnow, crawler harnesses, crankbaits trolled at night
Pro tip

Walleye eyes are adapted to low light, so the evening transition and windy, stained-water days ('walleye chop') are prime. Major solunar periods at dusk can be outstanding.

Freshwater

Northern Pike

Habitat
Weedy bays, cool northern lakes, slow rivers
Best season
Spring (shallow bays) and fall
Best time
Daytime ambushers; bright days are fine
Top baits
Spoons, inline spinners, swimbaits, quick-strike rigs
Pro tip

Use a wire or heavy fluorocarbon leader since pike teeth cut light line. Carry long-nose pliers or jaw spreaders and handle by supporting the belly, never the eye sockets.

Freshwater

Crappie

Habitat
Brush piles, standing timber, docks, bridge pilings
Best season
Spring spawn in shallows; winter suspended schools
Best time
Dawn, dusk and under lights at night
Top baits
Small jigs, live minnows under a slip float
Pro tip

Crappie school tightly and suspend. Find one and you've found many. Count your jig down and note the depth of every bite.

Freshwater

Bluegill & Sunfish

Habitat
Shallow weed edges, docks and ponds everywhere
Best season
Late spring to summer (spawning beds)
Best time
All day; best mid-morning to afternoon
Top baits
Worms, crickets, tiny jigs, small poppers
Pro tip

The best first fish for kids and new anglers: plentiful, aggressive and found almost everywhere. Use small hooks (size 8–10) and a light bobber.

Freshwater

Channel Catfish

Habitat
Rivers, reservoirs and ponds; holes, outside bends
Best season
Late spring to early fall
Best time
Night, dusk and rising water after rain
Top baits
Cut bait, chicken liver, nightcrawlers, stink bait
Pro tip

Catfish feed by smell and taste, so fresh bait matters. A slip-sinker rig on the bottom near a deep hole or channel edge is the classic, reliable setup.

Fresh & salt

Striped Bass

Habitat
Coastal rips, beaches, estuaries; large reservoirs (landlocked)
Best season
Spring and fall migrations
Best time
Night, dawn, moving tide
Top baits
Bunker and eels, bucktails, topwater plugs, soft plastics
Pro tip

Stripers feed hard on moving water. Plan around the tide changes, and on the coast match the size of the bait in the area.

Saltwater

Redfish (Red Drum)

Habitat
Shallow flats, marsh edges, oyster bars, jetties
Best season
Fall bull-red runs; year-round inshore
Best time
Moving tide, especially the first of the flood onto flats
Top baits
Gold spoons, soft plastics, cut mullet, live shrimp
Pro tip

Look for tailing fish in skinny water on a rising tide. A weedless gold spoon or a soft plastic on a light jighead tempts most reds.

Related guide →

Saltwater

Snook

Habitat
Mangrove shorelines, passes, bridges, beaches (subtropics)
Best season
Spring and summer; follow seasonal closures
Best time
Night around lights, moving tides, dawn
Top baits
Live pilchards and shrimp, soft-plastic jerkbaits, swimbaits
Pro tip

Snook ambush from structure on moving water. Snook seasons and slot limits are strictly managed, so check local rules before keeping one.

Related guide →

Saltwater

Spotted Seatrout

Habitat
Grass flats, potholes, deeper channels in winter
Best season
Spring and fall; winter in deep holes
Best time
Dawn and dusk, moving tide
Top baits
Popping cork with shrimp, soft plastics, topwater walkers
Pro tip

Drift grass flats in 1–2 m of water and cover ground until you find a school. Keep a fish in the water when releasing, as trout are delicate.

Related guide →

Saltwater

Flounder (Fluke)

Habitat
Sandy bottoms near inlets, channel edges, structure
Best season
Summer into fall migration
Best time
Moving tide, especially outgoing at inlets
Top baits
Bucktail jigs with strip bait, Gulp! grubs, live minnows
Pro tip

Flounder ambush from the bottom. Bounce a bucktail slowly along channel edges and give the fish a second before setting the hook.

Offshore

Mahi-Mahi (Dolphinfish)

Habitat
Blue water offshore; weed lines and floating debris
Best season
Late spring to summer
Best time
Daylight; follow birds and weed lines
Top baits
Trolled ballyhoo and skirted lures, cut bait, live pilchards
Pro tip

Find floating structure (weed lines, debris) and you'll usually find mahi. Keep a hooked fish in the water and the school will often stay close.

Related guide →

Offshore

Yellowfin Tuna

Habitat
Deep offshore water, temperature breaks, oil rigs, seamounts
Best season
Varies by region; often summer to fall
Best time
Dawn bites and around feeding birds
Top baits
Trolled spreader bars and cedar plugs, chunking, poppers
Pro tip

Yellowfin trips are usually long, full-day charters with heavy tackle. A good captain who watches water temperature and current breaks makes all the difference.
